## Count calibration specification before production

The statistic is the difference between post and pre means of quarterly count gaps expressed per included month, divided by the centered pre-period RMS (standard deviation) of those monthly gaps. Equivalently, dividing all gaps by the same training mean cancels in studentization. It is not a month-specific percentage-effect statistic. Each assignment repeats road-cell collapse, nuisance fitting, inner penalty selection, forward model comparison and final PPML/ASCM fitting. The corridor sums the inner rings, while headline selection retains the declared joint-ring rule. Simulated corridor alternatives affect the inner rings only; outer rings keep their no-effect means.

The count generator evaluates the fitted pre-only count model at synthetic controls. Future weather and holidays repeat pre-period month-of-year inputs; future weekends use calendar composition. Future common cell-month factors use the pre-period month-of-year average. These are transparent extrapolation assumptions, not observed post outcomes. Private/day/hour marks preserve the composition used by the estimators. Hour marks preserve peak/night/other/missing categories, not every hour of the future triple-difference analysis.

Stationary counts use cell-level gamma frailty calibrated to pre-model NB2 dispersion. The serial/seasonal scenario is an additional covariance stress: it uses shared unit and common lognormal shocks with combined log-intensity standard deviation `max(sqrt(log1p(phi)), 0.10)`, so aggregate variance need not equal the stationary generator's variance. The positive floor is a fixed sensitivity assumption, not an estimated dispersion parameter. Common persistence 0.4, seasonal amplitude 0.25 and equal common/unit log-variance shares are fixed stress assumptions. Unit persistence is estimated from the requested outcome's pre residuals. All latent processes evolve on the uninterrupted calendar before observations are omitted. Continued drift uses the requested outcome's own pre target/donor log-gap slope; private drift applies only to private marks. The proposed trend estimator refits treated-unit slopes inside every training fold and pseudo-assignment, carrying slope estimation uncertainty through the Monte Carlo experiment. This calibration conditions on the fitted data-generating parameters: refitting carries repeated-sample slope-estimation error, but does not propagate uncertainty in the generator's fitted slope or provide a slope confidence band or J1-equivalent interval.

The time reassignment fixes incomplete quarters and samples distinct, calendar-admissible assignments of complete quarters with the original dates and covariates attached. Assignment admissibility requires the fixed inner/outer forward-fold seasonal support. Thirty-nine reassignments plus identity give a minimum two-sided inclusive-tail p-value of 0.05. This coarse restricted test has no asserted exactness; its empirical size is the point of calibration. Production uses two hundred independent replications per cell, giving a Monte Carlo standard error about 0.0283 at power 0.8. Simulated hypotheses test zero effect. Nonzero-null inversion and confidence-interval coverage are not established by this experiment.

The predeclared size screen retains the earlier pragmatic tolerance of 0.075 at nominal 0.05 and reports binomial Monte Carlo uncertainty. A failed screen yields no valid MDE. Power uses a fixed magnitude grid of 0.15, 0.30, 0.50, 0.75 and 0.90 in each direction; the threshold must sustain at least 0.8 power at every larger grid point. Report intervals between grid points or an upper bound at the first grid point, never an exact off-grid minimum. The drift variant is reported separately and cannot automatically replace the original candidate.

For computational efficiency, a reassignment test may stop once both inclusive tail counts imply that rejection at the fixed level is impossible, even if every remaining statistic fell in the smaller tail. This gives exactly the same rejection decision as completing the fixed rank set, assuming those fits are defined. It does not give an exact p-value: incomplete ranks retain missing p-values, their bounds and the number of refits. No fitted model is reused across assignments. Power points are evaluated from the largest magnitude down; after the first point below the target, all smaller points fail the sustained-grid criterion regardless of their unobserved power, so they need not be run.

A case's proposed trend variant is evaluated only if its baseline continued-drift size fails the declared screen; that variant then receives null calibration in every scenario and power in its size-qualified scenarios. This is conditional evaluation of a proposal, never automatic adoption.
