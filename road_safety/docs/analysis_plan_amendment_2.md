## Estimators and forecast selection

For the stacked count panel, the PPML conditional mean is

`E[Y_zct(0)] = exp(alpha_zc + lambda_tc + s_z,q(t) + X_zt beta)`.

Here alpha is zone × cell type, lambda is month × cell type, and s is treated-unit × calendar-quarter-of-year seasonality. Fit the seasonality and nuisance controls on the pre-period only and freeze them for post estimation. Add one treated-unit × after-opening coefficient per ring or separately modelled target. Replace those coefficients with opening-aligned quarter leads/lags for the event study; use September–November 2023 as the reference. Use the same controls and data availability rules across estimator comparisons. No regression standard errors or normal/Wald tests support inference.

In pre-period holdouts, future month × cell effects cannot be extrapolated as if known: derive them from comparator outcomes in that held-out month while freezing the unit/cell and seasonality terms. The current code does this. Never use the held-out target to fit these terms. The evaluation is conditional forecasting using contemporaneous comparators, not an unconditional forecast of Quito traffic.

For the share outcome, PPML uses one aggregate numerator/exposure cell per unit-month: private-vehicle counts with log(all involved vehicles) as an offset. Do not condition this headline share on the realized crash-type mix, which can itself change because of the Metro. Zero-denominator observations have no observed share and contribute no Poisson exposure. The denominator defines the outcome, not a traffic control. A fractional/binomial specification is a secondary check. Report share effects in percentage points and numerator effects separately. `18_amend2_share_event.R` now validates this aggregate specification on the pre-period. Its ASCM comparator fits a quarterly Beta-half regularized share, `(private + 0.5)/(vehicles + 1)`, with finite rain/calendar/pico control factors removed/reinstated; seasonal variation remains in the quarterly share history. This explicitly assigns a prior mean to zero-exposure donor quarters for fitting; it is not a measured share. That regularization requires sensitivity/approval, and its presence is another qualification on provisional selection. Validation scores use actual held-out numerators and denominators, not the regularized observed share. Report predictions outside the unit interval; never silently clip them after selection.

The ASCM outcome is observed crashes divided by expected crashes under frozen pre-period local cell structure and common cell-specific time changes. **Correction to the requested citywide denominator:** common rate changes come from distant comparators only, and each donor's own rate benchmark excludes that donor. A denominator including treated outcomes would mechanically remove some of the Metro effect. This correction requires Leonel's explicit approval. A small fixed cell regularizer keeps zero baseline cells finite, without dropping parishes. The corrected ASCM implementation removes/reinstates only finite pre-fitted rain/calendar/pico control factors (and the separately proposed trend when enabled), then sums observed and expected counts to quarters before taking their ratio. Seasonal variation remains in its quarterly history. It does not divide by treated-season effects: a season with zero training crashes has a legitimate Poisson zero-mean boundary, but dividing positive held-out counts by that factor is undefined. PPML retains treated-season effects and their proved zero-mean limit. This explicit estimator correction replaces the earlier added shared seasonal preprocessing; it is not an observationally equivalent coding cleanup. The exposure and failed-run record are in `amendment2_continuation_execution.md`.

Use `augsynth` with ridge augmentation for individual target units and partially pooled `multisynth` for the rings. The current ring implementation fixes nu at one half, uses fixed-effects augmentation and no latent factors; its lambda penalizes the weights. Single-unit lambda is the ridge-augmentation penalty. These are not identical penalty parameters. Predictors are pre-period crash structure by type and hour, road length by class, 2022 population density and pico y placa exposure. The continuation supplies motor-road length in the full supplied historical OSM extract by class and census population/density predictors in `geographic_predictors.csv`, calculated for every registered unit. `parish_road_lengths.csv` additionally inventories road length by class for every DMQ parish, including those outside the comparator registry. Census DPA codes join the GeoQuito parish polygons. Non-parish population is the sum of parish populations allocated by intersection-area fraction, explicitly assuming uniform population within each parish. Use this approximation only as a predictor, never as a measured crash exposure or outcome denominator. The delivery LEEME documents municipal transcription and census-boundary limitations.

Use identical forward held-out quarters beginning March, June and September 2023. Train strictly before each quarter, separately for each pre start. Refit every transformation, sparse-cell rule, weekend share, control adjustment and predictor inside each fold. Tune the penalty on the last available training quarter, rebuilding its transformations from earlier months; use the largest penalty within five percent of minimum inner loss. The tested penalty grid is 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10. Record the short training histories and optimizer warnings.

Convert ASCM predictions to counts before comparing loss with PPML, pooled weights and equal weights. Divide each held-out quarter's error by three times that unit's **training monthly mean**; use the same scale for every method. Select on unrounded mean squared error. A method with a failed fold cannot win on a smaller sample. For rings use joint mean loss across all rings and the common folds. For each other unit/outcome select separately. **ASCM is headline if its loss is no worse than PPML's; otherwise PPML is.** Always show the other model beside it. Zero-volume donor baselines use an explicit small regularizer in the equal-weight benchmark rather than dropping a parish. Report the regularizer and distinguish that benchmark from strict unregularized indexing.

## Inference and the long-window problem

Main inference is intended to use time-based conformal inference on treated-unit residuals. The key assumption is stability of the untreated residual process after controls; donor count alone cannot justify it. Use [Chernozhukov, Wüthrich and Zhu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09089) for the dependence-aware framework.

The old plan's warning about a long post-period must be stated precisely: an all-period intercept can absorb part of a level shift, especially with a mean-absolute-residual statistic. Merely replacing absolute mean post residual by absolute post-minus-pre mean multiplies equal-length rotation statistics by a constant and does not solve the problem. The primary redesign candidate is **null-imposed symmetric refitting**. For a sharp constant proportional effect path delta greater than minus one, divide actual treated post counts by `1 + delta` to impose the null, rather than subtracting a percentage from counts. This is not a test of an unrestricted average-effect null. The current calibration exchanges complete three-month opening-aligned quarters, with incomplete quarters fixed and original dates attached. One- and six-month schemes are prospective sensitivities, not part of the current calibrated experiment. Presample thirty-nine distinct calendar-admissible reassignments plus identity. For each assignment evaluated before justified nonrejection stopping, refit the learner on its pseudo-pre sample, including scaling, cell collapse, tuning and seasonality; compute the post-minus-pre contrast in quarterly count gaps per included month, weighted by included months and divided by the centered pre-period standard deviation of those monthly gaps; use twice the smaller inclusive rank tail, capped at one. When the pre-period gap standard deviation is zero, report the test as undefined rather than introduce an effect-dependent floor. Preserve excluded months and unequal window exposure. The completed zero-null calibration is reported below; computational completion and a fine permutation grid do not establish finite-sample exactness.

The proportional sharp-null recipe above applies to count outcomes only. For the private-vehicle share, a percentage-point null path, its feasible bounded parameter set and the corresponding inversion remain to be specified and validated in the full-inference gate.

The continuation implements the full count learner-refitting procedure in `21_amend2_count_calibration.R` and `amend2_full_engine.R`; its status and empirical validity are reported separately from the historical prototype. The procedure tests the zero-effect null. Nonzero-null inversion and confidence-interval coverage remain unvalidated. `amendment2_continuation_execution.md` fixes the generator, candidate trend variant, calendar assignments, computational stopping rules and Monte Carlo precision before production. The prototype compares the legacy absolute-mean rule with equal-tail ranks of a pre-anchored contrast using equal-length blocks that do not cross missing months. The changed tail rule, not centering alone, changes its rejection behavior. Its Gaussian normalized-gap generator uses pre-period noise, serial dependence, seasonal variance and continued drift. It does not generate the multivariate count/vehicle process, repeat method selection or refit PPML/ASCM. Its conditional detectable shifts are percentages of the **pre-period mean**, not percentage effects on the future counterfactual. They cannot be the headline MDEs requested for the final design.
