## 2. Los Chillos timing

Script: `air_quality/code/local/diagnostics/loschillos_gapfill_timing.py`. Tables: `air_quality/output/local/diagnostics/pm25_gap/loschillos_timing_*.csv`. The classification rule was written into the script header before the first run; the script is committed with this report. The other seven stations always come from `PM2.5.xlsx`; the script asserts that the gap-fill file's copies of them are identical, so a whole-file clock offset cannot pass for a Los Chillos one.

**Verdict under the fixed rule: something else / unclear.** Within the rule's ±3 hours, the profile test gives the same best shift against both references (-3 hours, on the edge of the range). The rule still returns "unclear" because the lag test disagrees against March 2025, a month in which Los Chillos barely tracks the other stations. The level fits the station; the decimals and the unshifted daily shape do not.

a. **Weekday profiles and peaks** (`loschillos_timing_peaks.csv`, `loschillos_timing_profile_shift.csv`):

| Series | Los Chillos morning peak | Los Chillos evening peak | Other seven, morning | Other seven, evening |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gap-fill, 2025-01-13 to 25 | 10:00 | 12:00 | 07:00 | 13:00 |
| Own record, March 2025 | 09:00 | 20:00 | 07:00 | 12:00 |
| Own record, January to March 2024 | 08:00 | 18:00 | 07:00 | 12:00 |
| Own record, 2024-01-15 to 27 (same weeks) | 06:00 | 21:00 | 07:00 | 13:00 |

The evening peak is the maximum over 12:00 to 23:00. The gap-fill's "evening" maximum at 12:00 means its profile falls through the afternoon and has no evening rise.

The unshifted correlation of the gap-fill profile with the station's own profile is 0.007 against March 2025 and -0.156 against January to March 2024. Within ±3 hours, the best shift is -3 against both (0.466 and 0.438), on the edge of the range, so the true best may lie further out. In a side run over ±12 hours, added after code review, the best shift is +7 against March 2025 (0.60) but -4 against early 2024 (0.49), so the side run does not settle on one shift either. The station's own two periods agree with each other within one hour (best shift -1, correlation 0.57).

b. **Lagged correlation with the mean of the other seven** (`loschillos_timing_lags.csv`; a best lag of -k means Los Chillos's pattern comes k hours later):

| Series | Best lag in ±3 | Correlation at best | At lag 0 | Best lag in ±12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gap-fill | -3 (edge; -2 nearly tied, 0.4635) | 0.4647 | 0.3141 | -3 |
| Own record, March 2025 | -3 (edge) | 0.2756 | 0.1698 | +12 (edge) |
| Own record, January to March 2024 | -1 | 0.2727 | 0.2519 | -1 |

In March 2025 Los Chillos barely tracks the other stations (correlations 0.16 to 0.28 within ±3 hours), so its lag is poorly identified there. The audit found that REMMAQ's own Los Chillos also lagged the others in the two weeks before the outage, by one to two hours (best -2 at 0.4859, nearly tied with -1 at 0.4847; 0.3085 at lag 0), against two to three hours for the gap-fill. On this measure the gap-fill is not far from the station's record just before the hole, and unlike early 2024 (best -1).

c. **Level and spread** (`loschillos_timing_levels.csv`), all hours and weekday peak hours:

| Series | Hours | n | Mean | SD | Median | Los Chillos mean / other seven |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gap-fill | all | 311 | 13.60 | 7.30 | 12.15 | 1.010 |
| Gap-fill | weekday peak | 60 | 13.27 | 8.22 | 11.29 | 0.753 |
| Own record, March 2025 | all | 368 | 11.48 | 7.05 | 10.08 | 1.015 |
| Own record, March 2025 | weekday peak | 63 | 13.79 | 6.08 | 13.04 | 0.944 |
| Own record, same weeks of 2024 | all | 311 | 15.79 | 11.12 | 13.95 | 1.188 |
| Own record, same weeks of 2024 | weekday peak | 60 | 16.78 | 10.51 | 15.48 | 1.031 |
| Own record, January to March 2024 | all | 2,144 | 17.23 | 12.09 | 15.64 | 1.207 |
| Own record, January to March 2024 | weekday peak | 384 | 19.61 | 12.81 | 18.12 | 1.074 |

Over all hours, the gap-fill's level, spread and ratio to the other stations are close to March 2025. At the weekday peak hours the panel uses, it reads 25 percent below the other stations, against 6 percent below to 7 percent above in the station's own periods. This is consistent with a difference in daily shape.

d. **Decimals** (`loschillos_timing_decimals.csv`): 309 of the 311 gap-fill values have more than two decimals (at most five). In the station's own record, none of 368 (March 2025), 2,144 (January to March 2024) or 311 (same weeks of 2024) does.

**What this means.** The evidence is inconclusive. The column's level fits Los Chillos, but its decimals and its unshifted daily shape do not, and the tests neither confirm nor rule out a fixed time shift. It could be a differently processed or differently timed product, which questions 1 and 7 ask about. Using it would put a series with a different daily shape into the peak-hour window at a donor station. That is a missing-data choice to decide and write down before any re-run (your item 6).

## 8. Monitor coordinates

Script: `air_quality/code/local/diagnostics/monitor_distances.R`, run with the system `sf`, since `sf` is not in `renv.lock`. Tables: `air_quality/output/local/diagnostics/monitor_distances/`.

**Where the paper's distances come from.** Figure 1 reads `data/for_maps/Distancia_REMMAQ_Metro.gpkg`: monitor points in WGS 84 with a `HubDist` field in km, the distance to the nearest of the 15 points in `MetroStations.gpkg`. Table 2, Table 6, the descriptives and the panel use the same numbers, typed into `02_build_weekly_panels.R:49`, `11_descriptives.R:31-32`, `04b:49` and `04c:48`. My distances from the layer's coordinates reproduce `HubDist` and its nearest stop for every monitor. The difference is under 10 m, a projection scale effect of about 0.06 percent in UTM 17S, not noise. The four hard-coded vectors list San Antonio and Tumbaco the other way round from the layer. The paper's tables print them correctly, but the CSV columns and the panel's `dist_station` carry the swap.

**The coordinates are coarse and match the published ones.** Belisario, Centro, El Camal and Los Chillos are given to two decimals of a degree, about 1.1 km. These are the coordinates the Secretaría published: a 2010 table reproduced in Table 2 of a UDLA thesis gives Belisario as 78°29'24'' W, 0°10'48'' S (that is, -78.49, -0.18) and Centro as 78°30'36'' W, 0°13'12'' S (-78.51, -0.22). The other five monitors have four decimals.

**Documented sites.** CORPAIRE's "Características de la REMMAQ" (undated; CORPAIRE was the agency that ran the network before the Secretaría) places Belisario on the terrace of the Colegio San Gabriel administration building and Centro on the Radio Municipal terrace (García Moreno 751 y Sucre). In OpenStreetMap, the San Gabriel campus centroid is 0.89 km from the published Belisario point. Every point of the campus bounding box truncates to the published -78.49, -0.18, and none rounds to it, which supports the congestion session's reading that the published values were truncated. Centro cannot tell the two rules apart (its proxy point both truncates and rounds to the published value). For Centro, I used the Iglesia de la Compañía, which stands on the García Moreno and Sucre corner, as a proxy. It is the church's footprint, not the street corner.

4. **Belisario's role in M7 and in the San Antonio imputation.** M7 co-treats Centro and Belisario, and the San Antonio regression leaves both out as potentially treated. Both choices rest on Belisario being near the corridor. At 1.04 to 1.44 km it is still the second-nearest monitor, but "near" is weaker.
5. **The other monitors.** For Guamaní and everything farther out, nothing changes with a 1 km error. El Camal, not in the panel, sits across the 1 km line (1.21 km in the layer, 0.08 to 1.51 km within its cell).
