# Recorded crashes: corrected January 2022 start

Recorded crashes within 500 m of stations changed by an estimated **+9.0% relative to the comparison areas**, with a 95% confidence interval of **−7.1% to +27.9%**. The corrected analysis therefore leaves both a decline and an appreciable increase possible. It does not establish that the Metro improved or worsened road safety. The comparison covers December 2023–August 2026 against January 2022–November 2023. [Paired-start table](../../road_safety/output/amendment4_stage_b_2022/estimates.csv)

## What changed and what the results show

January 2022 restores Leonel's earlier decision to omit the COVID-19 recovery and the October–December 2021 recording block. The full January 2021-start results had already been seen when the instruction error was corrected. They remain beside the primary results as a sensitivity: its inner-ring all-crash estimate is **+19.0%** (4.2% to 36.0%). The difference between starts matters for interpretation; the correction does not erase the earlier results. [Correction record](../../road_safety/docs/amendment4_start_correction.md)

With the corrected start, the outer-ring all-crash estimate is **−1.6%** (−12.5% to 10.7%). Pedestrian estimates are **+36.7%** within 500 m (−8.6% to 104.6%) and **+5.7%** between 500 m and 1 km (−26.6% to 52.1%). These secondary estimates are descriptive and imprecise. [Paired-start table](../../road_safety/output/amendment4_stage_b_2022/estimates.csv)

For the primary outcome, adding valley areas gives **+5.8%** (−9.1% to 23.1%); using only comparisons entirely beyond 2 km of the line gives **−1.9%** (−16.2% to 14.7%). Parish clustering gives **+9.0%** (−8.4% to 29.8%). The checks also leave decreases and increases possible. [Paired-start table](../../road_safety/output/amendment4_stage_b_2022/estimates.csv)

The descriptive severity estimates are **+5.6%** for damage-only crashes (−10.6% to 24.8%) and **+12.7%** for injury or fatal crashes (−11.0% to 42.6%). Every estimable typology interval includes zero. VOLCAMIENTO is **Not estimable: too few events to fit**. [Typology and severity table](../../road_safety/output/amendment4_stage_b_2022/descriptive_types_severity.md)

## What this can and cannot rule out

This is mainly a comparison of the northern and southern **periphery with the central station spine**. Before opening, inner-ring all-crash estimates were mostly below the reference quarter, while outer-ring estimates were above it. Pedestrian estimates fluctuate widely. Their broad intervals cannot establish that the groups would have followed parallel proportional trends without the Metro. [All-crash figure](../../road_safety/output/amendment4_stage_b_2022/quarterly_all_crashes.png); [descriptive pedestrian figure](../../road_safety/output/amendment4_stage_b_2022/quarterly_pedestrian_descriptive.png).

The intervals describe uncertainty under the model's assumptions. They do not rule out different underlying trends, changes in reporting, development, feeder traffic or policies affecting areas differently. These are recorded crash counts, not risk per trip or a citywide effect. Every planned month is present in the delivery, which does not prove exhaustive reporting. Shared shocks across areas could still make clustered intervals too narrow. Intervals are pointwise, not simultaneous; zero-containing intervals are not evidence of no change. Tables record estimator omissions, and the figures protect sparse cells. Exact counts remain local.
